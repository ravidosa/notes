# Word Formation
mental lexicon: words stored in head
orthography: unit in writing system
	not always best way to define word (phonology, semantics, internal integrity)
	clitics: no prosodic independence
compound: word consisting of multiple works
	concatenation: stick together
	conversion: noun to verb (aka zero morph)
	truncation: make shorter
	acronym/abbreviation
	causative morpheme
	vowel alternation (stick vs stuck)
	analogy (airsick)
	back-formation: deleting suffix
	multiple levels: break up as tree
		parasynthesis: simultaneous multiple affixation
stress
	primary (only one) vs secondary
syntactic categories
	noun, verb, etc
	grammatical/morphosyntactic word: specified by grammar
	word-form: part of speech + usage
		lexemes abstract
			homophones: different lexemes with same pronunciation
			homonyms: written and pronounced same way
			polysemy: related meanings, same form
morphemes (morph = form)
	bound (can't be on own) vs free
	root/stem/base: most meaningful element
		can be bound
		base: part to which affix is attached
		root: base that cannot be split apart more
	[affix](derivation.md): added on (prefix vs suffix vs infix vs circumfix)
	minimal sign (unit of form and meaning)
	extended exponence: forms extended across elements
	discontinuous: meaning realized by multiple morphs
	monomorphemic: smallest meaningful unit
	allomorphs: many morphs, one morpheme
		conditioning affects distribution
			phonological: sound structure
			morphological: other morphemes
			lexical: meaning
		assimilation vs dissimilation
		complementary distribution
		governed by morphophonological rules (word-formation rule)
			redundancy rules
inflection (changes form/grammar) vs derivation (changes syntactic category/meaning)
opacity: difficulty to get meaning from constituent morphemes
	compositional: meaning =  function of meaning of parts (not opaque)
[productivity](productivity.md): ways to get new words
syncretism: different words, same word form
creativity: idioms
lexical phonology
	objections: not empirical, unsuccessful independent justifications
	alternatives
		giegerich: strata defined by bases
		fabb & plag: stratal behavior explained by selectional properties
		complexity based ordering theory: easy to parse should not precede hard to parse
			parsability gradient
word-based theoreies
	accounts for non-affixational derivation
