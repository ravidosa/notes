\problem{}
Let $X_i$ and $Y$ be binary variables, with $P(X_i = 1 | Y = 1) = \beta_{1i}$ and $P(X_i = 0 | Y = 1) = 1 - \beta_{1i}$. If $x_i = 1$:
\begin{align*}
    \beta_{1i}x_i + (1 - \beta_{1i})(1 - x_i) & = \beta_{1i} \cdot 1 + (1 - \beta_{1i})(1 - 1) = \beta_{1i}
\end{align*}
Instead, if $x_i = 0$:
\begin{align*}
    \beta_{1i}x_i + (1 - \beta_{1i})(1 - x_i) & = \beta_{1i} \cdot 0 + (1 - \beta_{1i})(1 - 0) = 1 - \beta_{1i}
\end{align*}
In either case, $P(X_i = x_i | Y = 1) = \beta_{1i}x_i + (1 - \beta_{1i})(1 - x_i)$.

Consider the sigmoid function, which maps the real line to $(0, 1)$:
\begin{align*}
    P(Y = 1 | X_1 = x_1, X_2 = x_2) & = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(w_1x_1 + w_2x_2 + b)}} \\
    P(Y = 0 | X_1 = x_1, X_2 = x_2) & = 1 - P(Y = 1 | X_1 = x_1, X_2 = x_2) \\
    & = 1 - \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(w_1x_1 + w_2x_2 + b)}} \\
    & = \frac{e^{-(w_1x_1 + w_2x_2 + b)}}{1 + e^{-(w_1x_1 + w_2x_2 + b)}} \\
    & = \frac{1}{1 + e^{(w_1x_1 + w_2x_2 + b)}}
\end{align*}
Now consider the hyperbolic tangent function, which maps the real line to $(-1, 1)$:
\begin{align*}
    f(x_1, x_2) = \tanh(e^{w_1x_1 + x_2x_2 + b})
\end{align*}
This modified function maps the real line to $(-1, 1)$:
\begin{align*}
    f(x_1, x_2) = \frac{1 + \tanh(e^{w_1x_1 + x_2x_2 + b})}{2}
\end{align*}
Then, letting $z = w_1x_1 + x_2x_2 + b$, note that:
\begin{align*}
    \frac{1 + \tanh(e^{z})}{2} & = \frac{1 + \frac{e^{2x} - 1}{e^{2x} + 1}}{2} \\
    & = \frac{\frac{2e^{2x}}{e^{2x} + 1}}{2} = \frac{e^{2x}}{e^{2x} + 1} \\
    & = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-2x}}
\end{align*}

The measures of lack of fit discussed are:

\textbf{Sum of squared errors:} If the data points are $(x_i, y_i)$ and the model predictions are $f(x_i)$, the sum of squared errors is $\sum_i (y_1 - f(x_i))^2$, and measures the deviation from the data points.

\textbf{Number of misclassifications:} For binary classification, the number of misclassifications is the sum of the number of false positives and false negatives.

\textbf{Sum of negative log likelihoods:} If the data points are $(x_i, y_i)$, the sum of negative log likelihoods is $\sum_i -\log P(Y  y_i | X = x_i)$, and measures the probability of deviating from the data points.
