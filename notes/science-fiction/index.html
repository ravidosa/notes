Science Fiction¶

The Origins of Science Fiction¶

called scientific romance unrealistic story involving science (fantastic voyages - jules verne) "science fiction" invented in 1929 by hugo gernsback utopian romance thomas more's utopia expedition to new world sci fi = fiction about future shift in worldview world is constantly changing (scientific revolution) greeks, etc thought world was fixed (plato's republic) possibility of imagining how future looks political and social change (revolutions) uniformitarianism (charles lyle), evolution (charles darwin) sci fi is literature of change - brooke landon

Readings¶

The Time Machine (H. G. Wells, 1895) chronic argonaut - other story by wells flatland, 4D (edwin abbott) time machine sidesteps convention from rip van winkle, etc the last man (mary shelley) everything told in retrospect evolution to eloi/morlocks (class divide) framing device of time traveler talking to representatives of victorian professional society time traveler's skepticism, prejudiced ideas about future

The Golden Age of Science Fiction¶

amazing stories by hugo gernsback scientifiction = science + fiction pedagogical approach to defining sci fi sci fi as vehicle for providing information sci fi hall of fame by robert silverberg nebula awards cognitive estrangement how different fictional world is from real world novum ethical vs unethical science, but ultimately good universe is big, humans are insignificant brain as metonym for cultural values

Readings¶

The Roads Must Roll (Robert A. Heinlein, 1940, in Robert Silverberg, ed., The Science Fiction Hall of Fame) conflict from uneven social enforcement technology is dangerous, but can be overcome functionalism, engineering approach to life failure of humanism vs scientism engineers have high social authority since they control machines The Cold Equations (Tom Godwin, 1954, in Robert Silverberg, ed., The Science Fiction Hall of Fame) powerlessness of humans against universe human desires irrelevant against laws of physics hard science fiction interrogating own reactions relative to laws cold equations are human laws and society society does not educate citizens, human lives expendable critique of masculinism Mimsy were the Borogoves (Louis Padgett (Henry Kuttner and C. L. Moore), 1943, in Robert Silverberg, ed., The Science Fiction Hall of Fame) radical breaks with human thought superhuman failure of human thought to describe phenomena, complete estrangement structure of scientific revolution (thomas kuhn) new paradigms of science development from childhood to adulthood different paradigms site of endless potential mind conditioned by growing up sci fi most transformative for children sci fi fans more prepared for change alice in wonderland song used as scientific instruction good structure, confusing semantics rex holloway = normalizing force of common society Microcosmic God (Theodore Sturgeon, 1941, in Robert Silverberg, ed., The Science Fiction Hall of Fame) pursuit of human intellectual perfection brain children = smart + product of doctor's brain transcendence through scientific thinking metaphor for social/technocratic organization microcosm of human civilization allegory for social progress science as politics kidder = science, banker = capitalism scientist as god, faith nietzschean scientific rationalism atom bomb, militarized science Nightfall (Isaac Asimov, 1941, in Robert Silverberg, ed., The Science Fiction Hall of Fame) discovery of physical limits of universe full maturation of sci fi, on another planet representations of different parts of social order speck in the universe psychological shock, epistemic transformation exposure to truth drives people mad sci fi tries to evoke sense of wonder plato's allegory of the cave, enlightenment Surface Tension (James Blish, 1952, in Robert Silverberg, ed., The Science Fiction Hall of Fame) exploring the beyond, outer space what lies beyond end of universe mythological framing (icarus) sci fi revises mythic structures using scientific ideas overcoming barrier of the surface with vehicle impenetrable barrier can be broken through kernel of skepticism infinite thresholds question level of achievements

From Golden Age to Counterculture¶

transitional time in genre sci fi overlooked in traditional dune is ambitious, wins awards, pulls out of ghetto

Readings¶

Dune (Frank Herbert, 1965) regime change, atrocities issues of colonialism people shaped by environmental forces beyond control more harsh, gritty antihero story, reactionary monomyth paul believes in own heroic capacity, subversion medievalization hero with a thousand faces (joseph campbell) structuralist interpretation linear progression, mastery of both worlds (human and fremen) calls out problems with heroes (superhero syndrome) moving from lower to higher orders of being line between human and animal, in group and out group intellectual rationality crossing categories is threatening butlerian jihad force human minds to develop machine as threat to autonomy liberate humanity by suppressing machine order of universe socially enforced hierarchy, overcoming limits protagonists are ruling class unpredictability, indeterminacy, fantasy of control gender, lineage, laws impose human logical construct onto universe, break down barriers bene gesserit manipulate social circumstances control reproduction, eugenics paul's blind spots connection between bene gesserit premonitions of future epigraphs foresees fate of planet, fremen jihad sees all futures, like computer extended inevitably progressive present ecological literacy anti war, environmentalism dichotomy with fremen back to land movement connections between organisms, planet as big organism, interconnectedness ourobouros of worm life cycle ecological elements have effects for whole system terraforming (jack williamson), ecological sustainability spice as metaphor for hallucinogens/psychedelics, oil Forbidden Planet (dir. Fred M. Wilcox, 1956) 2001: A Space Odyssey (dir. Stanley Kubrick, 1968)

The New Wave¶

ghetto of science fiction (harlan ellison) field of cultural production align with certain imagery illegitimate genre overwhelmingly, white, male, hetero, eurocentric genre globalization of sci fi racial, gender, class diversity sociological, philosophical questions interrogate golden age, criticize normative culture do away with genre conventions, postmodernism experimental sci fi metanarrative samuel delany, gay black man very experimental writing style explicit about sex, sexuality ursula k le guin, woman + anthropologist jewish woman working in sexist, anti-semitic culture

Readings¶

Aye, and Gomorrah (Samuel Delany, 1967) sexual desire for the outsider issues around gender, sexuality Nova (Samuel Delany, 1968) technologically advanced black civilization social systems reinforced by those who seek to oppose, hard to escape patterns taxonomy, categorization capitalist exploitation, mouse as social apparatus ashton clark as messianic figure, scientific religious cult skepticism towards scientific practice (tarot) etymological wordplay (nova) star, but also something that changes the way you think fragmentation of modern science illyrium reference to spice object of transcendental desire (holy grail, golden fleece) last sentence unended, fall into trap of repeating convention by filling in The Left Hand of Darkness (Ursula K. Le Guin, 1969) relations between humans and non humans alien invasion, classically vs scientific, anthropological approach ethnography as central observational authority, self-reflexive on colonial history understand historical development of genre shifting narrator, novelistic interiority (e. m. forster) prejudices, cultural biases question normative modes of gender, social order dispossession of women, octavia butler and margaret atwood human vs animal sexual dimorphism use of he vs they is gender physiological or psychological?, social construction estraven triggers examination of biases in genly crossing barriers bridges (keystone), organic connection through blood gender, kemmering divisiveness in planet unfamiliar with war, political tension movement from childhood to adolescence finding commonality across difference patriotism preventing love symbolic puns in genly's name (ai, eye, love, river) Blade Runner (dir. Ridley Scott, 1982)

Cyberpunk¶

apple macintosh commercial (1984 reference by ridley scott) mac as savvy, youth friendly tech vs ibm hackers: counter culture against totalitarianism, capitalism appropriate tech, science to subvert with political purpose, anarchism question authority, escape mortality GURPS incident cultural gap between revolution and crime cybernetics: science of controlling machines and organisms cyborgs with cybernetic implants (terminator, akira) whole earth catalog, wired

Readings¶

Preface to Mirrorshades (Bruce Sterling, 1986) integration of high tech and the underground Neuromancer (William Gibson, 1984) focus on post human case = flesh prison critique of humanist norms around gendering desire to decouple from body limitations of human existence neuromance (pun on romance) humans are remnants, embrace cyborg hybridity ethics vs morality vs law heist story globally distributed cyberspace, altered reality (fluid vs solid) decentralize and distribute information icebreakers, console cowboys (american ideal) highly explicit and sexual sensory experiences, synesthesia sexual vulnerability, anxiety around breaking down boundaries The Matrix (dir. Lily and Lana Wachowski, 1999)

The Work of Literature in an Age of Science Fiction¶

isolationist tendencies from trying to distinguish different modes of literary production blending (phillip k dick, margaret atwood) change in cultural value of sci fi literary vs genre fiction, prestige

Readings¶

Stories of Your Life and Others (Ted Chiang, 2002) understand superhuman/post human intelligence (flowers for algernon) patterns/truths of society story of your life sapir-whorf hypothesis, memory and time-space qualities of language the evolution of human science advancement of methodologies in science, hermeneutics division by zero inconsistency of logic sense of self and connection to mathematical knowledge bridge broken by empathy Everything Everywhere All at Once (dir. Daniel Kwan and Daniel Scheinert, 2022)

Afrofuturism¶

african-american thought framework, futuristic imagination for black people contextualized in cyberpunk (not transformative for race) connections to music (jazz), movies more sustainable, affirmative future african diasporic tradition mathmatical principles, mythology fictional african futurity and cultural production

Readings¶

Midnight Robber (Nalo Hopkinson, 2000) cyberpunk iconography in afro caribbean context references to black culture (toussaint, anansi) cybernetic protheses, diasporic connectivity (douen) written using caribbean dialect artificial intelligence (granny nanny) tan tan's costume as way of living tubman establishes new way to connect, continuity between human and AI Pumzi (dir. Wanuri Kahiu, 2010)

Science Fiction and Beyond¶

sci fi as discourse on relationship between science tech and society recursive, meta fiction

Readings¶

Aurora (Kim Stanley Robinson, 2015) critique of space colonization binds of capitalist exploitation anti-science sci fi pessimistic about space travel are generation ships viable? cultural amnesia, escapism, environmentalism elon musk, space tourism, climate change reversal of perspective, narrated by ship anthropocene: earth is a generation ship

Doing Things with Science Fiction¶

what can we do with science fiction? way of thinking about scientific experimentation vs generating social experiments synthesis: imagine the future, technical and social modes of experimentation
